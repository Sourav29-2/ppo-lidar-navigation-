========================================
FINAL DIAGNOSIS
========================================

Answer explicitly:

1. Are we currently giving PPO too many easy goals?
   YES. Over 33.3% of the benchmark is strictly EASY, and 36.7% has a completely clear direct path to the goal. These inflate the success rate (78% success on EASY) without testing complex obstacle avoidance.

2. Are RANDOM goals genuinely diverse in difficulty?
   NO. The RANDOM category skews heavily towards EASY (11 out of 20, or 55%). Only 7 of the 20 random goals even have a blocked direct path. Random coordinates in this environment frequently yield trivial open-space navigation.

3. What percentage of RANDOM goals actually require meaningful navigation?
   Only 35% (7 out of 20) require navigating around an obstacle (blocked direct path). 20% (4 out of 20) are classified as HARD.

4. Are the four-corner goals sufficiently challenging?
   It varies wildly. TOP_LEFT (100% blocked, HARD) and TOP_RIGHT (100% blocked, VERY_HARD) are genuinely difficult. However, BOTTOM_RIGHT is predominantly EASY (20% blocked) and BOTTOM_LEFT is MODERATE (60% blocked). They do not evenly distribute difficulty.

5. Is direct-path blockage strongly associated with failure?
   YES. The robot succeeds on 78.2% of clear paths, but crashes to a 36.8% success rate when the direct path is blocked. Collisions skyrocket from 10.9% to 45.3% when forced to detour.

6. Is detour difficulty associated with failure?
   YES. Success steadily drops from 78.0% (EASY) to 40.0% (MODERATE) to 32.6% (HARD). In VERY_HARD scenarios (high detour ratio or many obstacles), timeouts surge to 44.4% as the robot gets completely trapped.

7. What percentage of the benchmark represents the actual problem we are trying to solve?
   About 63.3% (the blocked path scenarios). If we only count MODERATE, HARD, and VERY_HARD, then 63.4% of the benchmark actually tests the core problem of breaking out of local minima and navigating around walls.

8. Is our current benchmark capable of distinguishing a genuinely better navigation policy from one that simply performs well on easy/open goals?
   Partially, but it is noisy. Because over 36% of the goals are trivial, a policy that degrades slightly on easy goals but masters complex detours might show a lower overall success rate, masking genuine navigation improvements.

9. What is the biggest weakness in our current goal distribution?
   The heavy reliance on naive RANDOM and static corner coordinates yields too many trivial straight-line tasks. We are evaluating "driving forward" just as often as we are evaluating "complex obstacle avoidance".

10. Based ONLY on this analysis, what kind of curriculum should we consider next?
   We should implement a heavily skewed curriculum that biases goal generation toward blocked direct paths, forcing detours. RANDOM goals should be structurally verified to ensure the direct path intersects a wall/obstacle before being fed to the agent, eliminating trivial "open corridor" goals from training.
